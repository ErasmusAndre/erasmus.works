My own cloud.

This is Erasmus.works — the place I host my own digital infrastructure. My photos, my files, my documents and my home automation all live on a small Kubernetes cluster, in a 10-inch rack I cut from aluminium extrusion and finished with mounts I designed and printed.

Nothing here is clicked into place by hand. Every service, every setting and every update is written down in one public Git repository, and the cluster keeps itself in step with it. Building over buying — and the whole thing, software and aluminium, is open source.

Services

What is actually running.

Photos go to Immich instead of Google Photos, files to Nextcloud instead of Dropbox, notes and documents to Docmost. The flat itself runs on Home Assistant.

One account signs into all of them — Authentik handles that — and none of it needs an open port on my router. Grafana is where I go to see whether any of it is unhappy.

Software

Every cluster change is a pull request.

Argo CD is the only thing that touches the cluster. Renovate opens the dependency PRs, merging one is the deploy, and a rebuild from bare metal is a talosctl apply-config away. No kubectl apply from a laptop, no state that exists only in someone's shell history.

What the cluster is made of.

  • Talos Linux Immutable node OS
  • Kubernetes Runs the containers
  • Argo CD Applies what Git says
  • Renovate Opens update PRs
  • Envoy Gateway Ingress and routing
  • MetalLB LoadBalancer IPs
  • ED ExternalDNS DNS from manifests
  • Cloudflare Tunnel, no open ports
  • Longhorn Persistent volumes
  • CloudNativePG In-cluster Postgres
  • VS VolSync Scheduled backups
  • External Secrets Secrets from Bitwarden
  • Prometheus Metrics and alerts
  • VictoriaLogs Log storage
  • Fluent Bit Log shipping

Hardware

Ten units, cut from four metres.

The rack is 2020 aluminium extrusion, slot 6 throughout: 220 mm wide, 240 mm deep, 445 mm tall. Twelve pieces come out of four one-metre lengths with 380 mm spare for saw kerf and one mistake. Machines that were never meant to be racked get 3D-printed mounts, published as STEP and 3MF under CERN-OHL-S.
